How to Choose Inserts for Cosmetic Packaging

EVA foam, folding paperboard, molded pulp, sponge, satin or velvet-covered EPE: compare how each material protects and presents your product.

Published November 27, 2026

The insert is the part of a cosmetic box that customers may notice only when it goes wrong. If a perfume bottle moves during delivery, a nail polish cap rubs against the lid, or a serum sits at an angle, the outer box can look beautiful and still feel poorly made.

There is no single best insert material. A rigid glass bottle, a lightweight tube and a mixed gift set need different levels of support. The right choice also depends on the box structure, presentation, order quantity and shipping route.

Quick recommendationChoose EVA or EPE when accurate cavities and firm positioning matter. Use folding paperboard when a lighter, lower-volume structure can do the job. Consider molded pulp when a formed paper-based solution fits the product and brand direction. Satin and velvet coverings improve presentation, but the supporting structure underneath still determines protection.

1. Satin Fabric Inserts for a Softer Luxury Presentation

Satin is selected mainly for presentation. The fabric creates soft folds around bottles and jars and works well for limited editions, fragrance sets and beauty gifts. It can be developed for either a foldable magnetic box or a conventional non-foldable magnetic rigid box.

The important detail is what sits below the fabric. Satin alone does not lock a heavy glass bottle in place. The arrangement may need a shaped base, board platform or foam support, depending on the products and transport conditions. A physical sample helps confirm how the fabric gathers and whether the products remain stable when the box is moved.

2. Folding Paperboard Inserts Save Space

A folding paperboard insert is cut and creased from card, then assembled into platforms, slots or compartments. It is a practical option when the product does not need thick foam cushioning. Because the structure can be delivered flat, it may take considerably less space than a pre-formed insert.

Paperboard also accepts printing, so the insert can carry instructions, product names or brand graphics. The trade-off is that the fit depends on well-designed locking tabs and accurate folds. Heavy glass bottles may need reinforced walls, double layers or a different material.

3. EVA Foam for Precise Product Positioning

EVA is firmer than soft sponge and can be cut into clean, repeatable cavities. It is often chosen for nail polish, perfume, essential oils and cosmetic tools where products must stay upright and separated.

Density and thickness matter as much as the material name. If the cavity is too tight, the customer struggles to remove the bottle. If it is too loose, the item can rattle or rotate. Finger notches, pull ribbons and clearance around caps should be considered during sampling.

4. Sponge Foam for Softer Cushioning

Sponge is softer and more compressible than EVA. It can absorb light pressure and accommodate shapes that benefit from a gentler surface. That softness also means it may not hold a tall or heavy bottle as firmly.

When comparing samples, check rebound, odor, surface dust, cavity accuracy and how the material behaves after compression. The word “foam” is not a sufficient specification: density, color, thickness and any surface covering should appear in the quotation.

5. Velvet-Covered EPE for Lightweight Presentation

EPE, often called pearl foam, is lightweight and resilient. Covering it with velvet or another fabric improves the visible surface and hides the raw foam texture. This combination is frequently used for perfume, oil, jewelry and presentation packaging.

The fabric must be applied cleanly around corners and narrow cavities. For bottles with very small necks or complex outlines, confirm whether the selected covering can follow the shape without wrinkles or exposed edges.

6. Where Molded Pulp Fits

Molded pulp is formed from fiber into a three-dimensional tray. It can be a good direction when the brand prefers a natural paper appearance and wants to reduce mixed-material components. It is useful for jars, bottles and sets when the product geometry works with the forming process.

It should not be treated as a direct visual substitute for precision-cut EVA. The surface is more textured, tooling requirements differ, and dimensional tolerances must be checked against the product. Ask for the fiber source, color, wall thickness and any coating rather than relying on a broad “eco-friendly” claim.

What to Check During Sampling

  • Fit the actual production product, not only a drawing.
  • Turn and lightly shake the closed box to check movement.
  • Confirm finger space and removal without pulling on caps or pumps.
  • Check that bottles remain below the lid and do not press against it.
  • Inspect fabric-covered edges, corners and narrow cavities.
  • Test the complete packed box for the planned shipping route.
  • Confirm material, density, thickness and color in the approved specification.

Cost, MOQ and Shipping Considerations

Insert cost depends on material, thickness, cavity count, cutting or forming method, covering fabric and assembly time. A simple folding paperboard platform is usually a different production job from a multi-layer EVA insert covered in velvet.

Our normal starting quantity for most custom packaging projects is 200 pieces per size and design. Selected 100-piece projects may be possible at a higher unit price, while 500 pieces is usually more cost-effective when the product has repeat sales. Tooling or molding requirements can also affect the practical MOQ for a specific insert.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which insert is best for glass cosmetic bottles?

A precisely cut EVA or EPE insert is often suitable when glass bottles need firm positioning. The final choice should be confirmed through a fitted sample and transit testing for the actual product.

Can a paperboard insert fold flat for shipping?

Yes. A paperboard insert can be designed as a die-cut folding structure and shipped flat before final assembly, reducing storage and freight volume compared with many pre-formed inserts.

Can satin fabric be used inside a foldable magnetic box?

Yes. Satin fabric arrangements can be developed for both foldable and non-foldable magnetic rigid boxes, although the anchoring method and assembly details differ by structure.

What is the difference between sponge and EVA foam?

Sponge is softer and compresses more easily, while EVA is generally firmer and can be cut into more precise cavities. Density, thickness and product weight must still be specified.

Is molded pulp suitable for luxury cosmetic packaging?

Molded pulp is useful when recyclability, a natural appearance and formed protection matter. Its surface and dimensional precision differ from EVA, so it should be evaluated against the desired presentation and product tolerance.

What information is needed to quote a custom insert?

Provide product dimensions, weight, photographs or samples, bottle orientation, box size, preferred material, order quantity and destination country. Fragile items may also require drop or transit testing.
